Racing to Confirm Trump's Judges: Did Democrats Do the Same in 2024?

Democrats won control of the Senate on Nov. 3, 2026. Until the new Senate takes office on Jan. 3, 2027, Republicans hold the majority, and they are moving to confirm as many of President Trump's judicial nominees as they can. At a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing on Nov. 10, Sen. Dick Durbin said the chairman is setting aside the committee's long-standing practices. Chairman Chuck Grassley answered that Democrats did the same in the 2024 lame duck. The confirmation counts match the chairman's number, and both parties have used a lame duck to confirm judges after losing an election; whether past chairmen also shortened the committee's timelines is not settled by the public record this file has found.

  1. Exhibit A

    The Charge

    Sen. Durbin lists four committee practices he says the chairman is setting aside.

  2. Exhibit B

    The Answer

    Chairman Grassley: Democrats did the same in recent lame ducks.

  3. Exhibit C

    The Precedent

    Both parties have confirmed judges after losing an election: 22 in 2024, 13 in 2020.

  4. Exhibit D

    The Hearing Itself

    Questions to Fifth Circuit nominee Todd Butler, from both sides.

  5. Exhibit E

    The Brakes

    Two outgoing Republicans can slow the plan.

The timeline

  1. Nov. 3, 2020President Trump loses the election. The Republican Senate goes on to confirm 13 more of his district and appeals court judges before that Congress ends.
  2. Nov. 5, 2024Democrats lose the White House and the Senate. The Democratic Senate goes on to confirm 22 judges in the lame duck.
  3. Nov. 13, 2024Sen. Durbin, then the committee's chairman, gives a floor speech his office titles "We must confirm every possible federal judge in the lame duck session."
  4. Nov. 3, 2026Democrats win control of the Senate.
  5. Nov. 10, 2026Senate Judiciary Committee business meeting and nominations hearing; Sen. Durbin and Chairman Grassley dispute the committee's practices.
  6. Nov. 12, 2026First confirmation of the lame duck: Gregory Cook, to a federal trial court in Alabama.
  7. NextAnother nominations hearing is scheduled for Nov. 18. The new Senate takes office on Jan. 3, 2027. This file stays open: it will read the confirmation votes against what was said on Nov. 10.

Open questions

  1. If a norm is set aside each time a majority is about to lose power, is it still a norm?
  2. The blue slip gives a single senator a say over a lifetime appointment. Which way will each side argue about it in January?
  3. Is either party willing to agree on a rule now, before it knows who it will be used against?

Sources & method

How a file is built

Every video follows the same discipline. The structure changes with the story. The standard does not.

  1. The record

    We start from primary material: committee video, court orders, official reports and transcripts.

  2. The exhibits

    Each piece of evidence gets a letter. You always know what you are looking at and where it came from.

  3. The timeline

    Dates go on one line, so the sequence of events is clear before anyone interprets it.

  4. The open questions

    Where the record stops, we say so. Allegations are called allegations.
