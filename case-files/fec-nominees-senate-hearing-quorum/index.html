Case File #015

FEC Has Had No Quorum for 18 Months: Two Nominees Faced Senate Panel

On Tuesday, Nov. 17, 2026, the Senate Rules Committee, with Sen. Bill Hagerty (R-Tenn.) leading the hearing, heard from Ashley Stow and Andrew Woodson, President Trump's nominees to the Federal Election Commission. The commission has six voting seats and, by law, no more than three members from one party. Many of its main powers need four votes: rules, advisory opinions, lawsuits, investigations and the first finding on a complaint. In a court filing the FEC says it has been without a quorum since May 1, 2025, after Commissioner Allen Dickerson's term ended; Republican Trey Trainor resigned that October, leaving two Democrats, Shana Broussard and Dara Lindenbaum. The Congressional Research Service calls it the fourth loss of a quorum and the longest. It also says President Trump reportedly removed Democratic commissioner Ellen Weintraub in January 2025; she said the letter was "purporting to remove me," and the research service says the legal question is beyond its report. We found no court ruling on it. If both nominees are confirmed, the commission would have four members, two Democrats and two Republicans, which is a quorum. Woodson said that with only four commissioners, if a recusal is triggered, "things would languish at the agency," and Stow agreed that every action with four commissioners, "for the most part, must be unanimous." Two of the six seats, the one Weintraub held and the one Sean Cooksey held, have no nominee as of Nov. 26. The committee has noticed a business meeting for Dec. 1, 2026, at 12:15 p.m. to consider the two nominations; no date is set for a vote of the full Senate.

The evidence

Exhibits

  1. Exhibit A

    The law

    The FEC has six voting members, appointed by the president and confirmed by the Senate; by statute no more than three may belong to the same party (52 U.S.C. 30106). Four affirmative votes are required to bring or defend a lawsuit, issue advisory opinions, make rules, conduct investigations, and for the first finding on a complaint that there is reason to believe the law was broken (30107, 30109). The FEC's own site says at least four votes are required for any official Commission action. Sen. Deb Fischer (R-Neb.) asked nominee Ashley Stow why the balance matters.

  2. Exhibit B

    The quorum

    At the start of 2025 all six seats were filled. Republican Sean Cooksey resigned effective Jan. 20, 2025. The Congressional Research Service says President Trump reportedly removed Democratic chair Ellen Weintraub effective Jan. 31; she wrote that the letter was "purporting to remove me," and the research service says the legal question is beyond its report. We found no court ruling. Republican Allen Dickerson left Apr. 30, 2025, at the end of his term. In a court notice the FEC says that as of May 1, 2025 it has been without a quorum; Sen. Hagerty said "since April," the same event. Republican Trey Trainor resigned Oct. 3, 2025, leaving Democrats Shana Broussard and Dara Lindenbaum. The research service calls this the fourth loss of a quorum and the longest; the earlier ones lasted roughly six to nine months. Sen. Alex Padilla (D-Calif.) and Sen. Mark Warner (D-Va.) criticized the removal at the hearing; Warner said that, to his knowledge, no president had removed a member before, and we found no earlier removal but also no neutral official source that says so outright.

  3. Exhibit C

    What the FEC cannot do

    The agency has not shut down: staff still review campaign reports and accept complaints. In an April 2026 statement Commissioner Broussard wrote that without a quorum the FEC has not been able to "resolve enforcement and audit matters, implement rules, issue advisory opinions, and litigate most cases." In court it has filed notices that it is without the authority to argue the merits of cases against it. Criminal enforcement by the Justice Department is not affected, and the law stays on the books; the research service says it may be enforced once a quorum returns. Stow described an enforcement docket of over 300 matters.

  4. Exhibit D

    The nominees

    The White House sent both names to the Senate on Feb. 11, 2026. Ashley Stow, of Texas, is nominated to the seat Trainor left, for a term ending in April 2029; she said she has been at the FEC since 2020. Andrew Woodson, of Virginia, is nominated to Dickerson's seat, for a term ending in April 2031; the committee biography says he is a partner at Wiley Rein and was counsel to FEC Chairman Lee Goodman. Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith (R-Miss.) asked how they would handle cases involving people they used to work for.

  5. Exhibit E

    What senators asked

    Hagerty asked about AI in campaign content; Stow pointed to the commission's September 2024 vote not to open a new rule and its finding that the existing ban on fraudulent misrepresentation is "technology neutral." Sen. Shelley Moore Capito (R-W.Va.) asked how the nominees would work through the backlog; Woodson pointed to foreign money, which federal law already bars. Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-Minn.) described mailers from a sender she said she cannot identify; Stow called "dark money" imprecise and said changes beyond that would take Congress. Warner asked whether the public should know who pays influencers; Stow would not comment on a pending rule, which we did not confirm. Capito called the FEC website terrible; Padilla raised its reported late-October outage, which news reports tied to an attempted breach.

  6. Exhibit F

    Independence

    Padilla asked both nominees whether a federal officeholder may legally control a super PAC, pointing to MAGA Inc. Both declined to answer, saying complaints on the subject had been filed and it would be inappropriate to discuss something that could come before them. Padilla then asked whether they had discussed campaign finance law, or any specific question, with the White House or people at MAGA Inc.; Stow: "Senator, I have not." Woodson: "I also have not." Warner asked a broader version. Padilla said he would send more questions in writing.

  7. Exhibit G

    The arithmetic

    If both are confirmed, the FEC would have four members, two Democrats and two Republicans. That is a quorum; the FEC says so in court and so does the research service. Capito asked whether every decision would then have to be unanimous, and Stow agreed that every action with four commissioners, "for the most part, must be unanimous." Woodson said a single recusal could leave things to languish. Two seats would remain empty: the one Weintraub held and the one Cooksey held. As of Nov. 26, 2026, no nominee has been named for either. Padilla said the White House refused to nominate for the Democratic seat; the record shows only that none has been named. Broussard is already serving past her term, and Lindenbaum's term ends in April 2027; members may stay until a successor takes office.

Charges in an indictment are allegations, not findings of guilt. Statements by senators and officials are reported as their statements.
