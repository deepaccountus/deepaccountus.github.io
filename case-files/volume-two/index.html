Case File #001

Volume Two: The Jack Smith Report They Refused to Even Vote On

Special Counsel Jack Smith's final report came in two volumes. The first was released. The second, on the classified documents case, was not: a federal judge barred the Justice Department from sharing it. On November 10, 2026, the chairman of the Senate Judiciary Committee ruled a request to vote on a subpoena for it out of order.

The evidence

Exhibits

  1. Exhibit A

    The Indictment

    In June 2023, a federal grand jury in Florida charged Donald Trump with willfully retaining national defense information and with conspiring to obstruct justice. The indictment alleged that boxes of documents, some marked top secret, were stored at Mar-a-Lago in a ballroom, a bathroom and shower, an office, a bedroom and a storage room, and that boxes were moved before his own lawyer searched that storage room. A later, superseding indictment alleged an attempt to have security camera footage deleted. These are allegations. Mr. Trump pleaded not guilty and was never convicted.

  2. Exhibit B

    The Lock

    In July 2024, Judge Aileen Cannon dismissed the case, ruling that Jack Smith's appointment as special counsel was unconstitutional. After the 2024 election, the appeal against Donald Trump was dropped. Volume One of the final report was released in January 2025. Volume Two was not: Judge Cannon barred the Justice Department from releasing, sharing or transmitting it outside the department, and that order later became permanent. Her reasoning, as read aloud by Chairman Grassley: the defendants were never convicted, they keep the presumption of innocence, and release would be a "manifest injustice." American Oversight and the Knight First Amendment Institute have appealed the order to the Eleventh Circuit.

  3. Exhibit C

    The Man Who Wrote It

    On September 29, 2026, Jack Smith testified before the Senate Judiciary Committee and stood by both of his investigations. Republican senators focused largely on subpoenas for lawmakers' phone records. On the classified documents case there was a limit. As Sen. Dick Durbin said in his opening statement, Smith himself is enjoined from providing information about that investigation unless the Justice Department makes it public.

  4. Exhibit D

    November 10

    At a committee business meeting, Sen. Adam Schiff asked for a roll-call vote on a subpoena to the Justice Department for Volume Two. Chairman Chuck Grassley ruled the request out of order, citing the chairman's control of the agenda, Judge Cannon's order and the pending appeal. No vote was held. Schiff's reply: "We can vote on this now, or we can vote on this in January, in which I think a subpoena will be issued."

Charges in an indictment are allegations, not findings of guilt. Statements by senators and officials are reported as their statements.

The timeline

  1. August 2022The FBI searches Mar-a-Lago.
  2. June 2023The indictment.
  3. July 2024Judge Cannon dismisses the case.
  4. November 2024Donald Trump wins the presidency. The case against him is dropped.
  5. January 2025Volume One is released. Volume Two is blocked.
  6. Sept. 29, 2026Jack Smith testifies, but cannot discuss Volume Two.
  7. Nov. 3, 2026Democrats win control of the Senate.
  8. Nov. 10, 2026The request for a vote is ruled out of order.
  9. January 2027A new Senate majority. An appeals court hearing on the order is set for January, according to both senators.

Open questions

  1. If the investigation was as flawed as its critics say, why not let the full report show it?
  2. Chairman Grassley has released records he says expose problems with Smith's team. If the goal is the complete picture, what is the harm in releasing the document that tells the other side?
  3. Does the presumption of innocence also protect documents from daylight?
  4. If a subpoena comes in January, will the Justice Department hand the volume over?
