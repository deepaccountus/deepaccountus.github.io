SEC Nominee on 401(k)s: "Absolutely Zero Allocation" to Alternatives

On Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026, the Senate Banking Committee, chaired by Sen. Tim Scott (R-S.C.), heard from five nominees, including two for the Securities and Exchange Commission: Steven Levine and Kristin Johnson, a law professor. The SEC has five commissioners by law, no more than three from one party; its website lists two, Chairman Paul Atkins and Commissioner Mark Uyeda. Levine would take the seat last held by Republican Hester Peirce, and Johnson the seat last held by Democrat Caroline Crenshaw. Levine said, under oath, that retirement savers in private 401(k) plans have "absolutely zero allocation" to alternative assets. A 2025 executive order says every American preparing for retirement "should have access to funds" that include alternative assets when the plan's fiduciary decides that access is appropriate; the Labor Department proposed a rule with a "safe harbor" on Mar. 31, 2026, and as of Nov. 27 we found no final rule. Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) asked four financial nominees whether private credit could pose serious risk to the financial system; the answers differed. The SEC's own releases show 583 enforcement actions in fiscal 2024 and 348 in fiscal 2026, with money ordered down from $8.2 billion to about $1.1 billion; single large cases swing those totals, and money returned to investors rose from about $262 million to about $731 million between fiscal 2025 and 2026. Senators also raised scams aimed at older savers. As of Nov. 27, the official nomination records list the latest action as "Hearings held," we found no committee vote scheduled, and one of the five SEC seats has no nominee.

The timeline

  1. Aug. 7, 2025President Trump signs Executive Order 14330 on access to alternative assets in 401(k) plans.
  2. Aug. 12, 2025The Labor Department withdraws a 2021 statement that had urged caution about private equity in 401(k) plans.
  3. Mar. 31, 2026The Labor Department proposes a rule with a safe harbor for plan fiduciaries (91 FR 16088).
  4. Nov. 10, 2026The SEC publishes its enforcement results for fiscal year 2026.
  5. Nov. 12, 2026The White House sends the nominations of Steven Levine and Kristin Johnson to the Senate.
  6. Nov. 19, 2026Senate Banking Committee nominations hearing; the official records list the latest action as hearings held.
  7. Nov. 20, 2026Deadline (noon) for senators' written questions to the nominees.
  8. Nov. 24, 2026Date by which the nominees were asked to answer in writing; we have not seen the answers.

Open questions

  1. Will the Banking Committee vote on the two SEC nominations, and will the full Senate act? We found no committee vote scheduled as of Nov. 27, 2026.
  2. What will the nominees' written answers say? They were asked to answer by Nov. 24; we have not seen them.
  3. Will the Labor Department finalize its 401(k) rule? As of Nov. 27, 2026, we found no final rule in the Federal Register.
  4. Will anyone be nominated to the fifth SEC seat? We found no nomination for it.
  5. If the Senate has not confirmed the nominees by Jan. 3, 2027, will the President send them again?

Sources & method

How a file is built

Every video follows the same discipline. The structure changes with the story. The standard does not.

  1. The record

    We start from primary material: committee video, court orders, official reports and transcripts.

  2. The exhibits

    Each piece of evidence gets a letter. You always know what you are looking at and where it came from.

  3. The timeline

    Dates go on one line, so the sequence of events is clear before anyone interprets it.

  4. The open questions

    Where the record stops, we say so. Allegations are called allegations.
