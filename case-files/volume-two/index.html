Case File #001

Jack Smith's Volume Two: The Senate Committee Vote That Didn't Happen

Special Counsel Jack Smith's final report came in two volumes. The first was released. The second, on the classified documents case, was not: a federal judge barred the Justice Department from sharing it. On November 10, 2026, the chairman of the Senate Judiciary Committee ruled a request to vote on a subpoena for it out of order.

Subject
The unreleased half of the Special Counsel's final report
Exhibits
A through E
Status
Published Nov. 15, 2026

The evidence

Exhibits

  1. Exhibit A

    The Promise

    On September 29, 2026, Jack Smith testified under oath before the Senate Judiciary Committee. Sen. Dick Durbin explained the ground rules: Judge Aileen Cannon has enjoined the release of Volume Two, and Smith himself is enjoined from providing information about that investigation unless the Justice Department makes it public. Much of the hearing concerned a different issue, the records Smith's office obtained on members of Congress. Chairman Chuck Grassley said the returns included the content of messages; that claim is the chairman's own, and this file has not seen the records behind it. Under questioning from Sen. Durbin, Smith described those records as toll records, which he called non-content records. Asked by Sen. Adam Schiff about Volume Two, Smith said he would not speak about anything that would even imply what is in the report, to stay in compliance with the court's order. Schiff then said he would make a motion at the next business meeting to subpoena the report.

  2. Exhibit B

    The Motion

    On November 10, 2026, at a committee business meeting, Sen. Schiff moved for a vote on a subpoena for Volume Two, directed at the Justice Department, and asked for a roll call vote under the committee's rules. In his statement he said that ten members of the committee had requested the report more than a year earlier, that the department already holds a final, redacted version, and that the court's order rested on a finding that Congress had shown no formal interest. Those three points are Sen. Schiff's own account; this file has not seen the documents behind them. What is on the record is the request itself.

  3. Exhibit C

    The Ruling

    Chairman Grassley ruled the request out of order. His reasons, as he gave them at the meeting: the chairman sets the agenda; Judge Cannon's order blocks the release of Volume Two outside the Justice Department; litigation over that order is before the Eleventh Circuit, with oral arguments he said are scheduled for January 12; and a fight over a committee subpoena would not finish before the court rules. He read from Judge Cannon's order: the defendants "still enjoy the presumption of innocence held sacrosanct in our constitutional order," and "manifest injustice" would result from releasing the report. He also predicted that, "if past is prologue, Volume Two is assuredly filled with allegations benefiting the special counsel's political positions." That is the chairman's prediction about a document that has not been released, and he called the attempt to force its release "premature." The indictment's claims are allegations. Mr. Trump pleaded not guilty and was never convicted on these charges.

  4. Exhibit D

    The Rule Book

    Sen. Schiff replied by citing the committee's rules. Rule 9, he said, lets the committee subpoena records by a vote of the committee. Rule 4, he said, requires a roll call vote when there is an objection to bringing a matter to a vote. He argued that denying the vote would set a precedent Republicans would have to live with when they are in the minority: "You'll be taking a position that when you're in the minority, you have no right to a vote on a subpoena." This is Sen. Schiff's reading of the rules; the chairman did not accept it.

  5. Exhibit E

    The Answer

    Chairman Grassley answered: "the chairman does not have your motion on the agenda, so it's out of order. If the chairman next year puts it on the agenda, then you'll deal with it at that time." No vote was held, and the committee moved on.

Charges in an indictment are allegations, not findings of guilt. Statements by senators and officials are reported as their statements.

Open questions

  1. The chairman says the full report would be filled with one-sided allegations. If that is true, what is the argument against letting the public read it, and then answering it?
  2. The presumption of innocence is real, and it matters. Earlier special counsel reports, on the Russia investigation and on President Biden's handling of classified documents, were made public. Judge Cannon drew a line here because charges were filed and never resolved. Can that line be debated without seeing what is on the other side of it?
  3. The report was written by federal prosecutors, about evidence gathered by federal agents, and paid for by the public. What would it take for the public to be allowed to read it?
  4. If Volume Two holds nothing new, releasing it would cost the public nothing. A federal judge has ruled that it would cost the defendants something. That is the line the appeals court will be asked to test.
