CISA Nominee on Jan. 6: "I Don't Believe It Was an Attack"

On Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026, the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee, chaired by Sen. Rand Paul (R-Ky.), with Sen. Gary Peters (D-Mich.) as ranking member, considered thirteen nominations in two panels. This file focuses on two. Michael LoSapio, chief information security officer at Palantir Technologies, is nominated to lead CISA, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, which has had no Senate-confirmed director since January 2025. Eric Ueland, deputy director for management at the White House budget office, is nominated to be Comptroller General, head of the Government Accountability Office, for a fifteen-year term. Asked whether there was an attack on the Capitol on Jan. 6, 2021, Mr. LoSapio first said "I don't know," then "I don't believe it was an attack." Mr. Ueland said: "I'm not here as a plant, a stooge, a lackey for anybody." Democrats criticized the process that produced the GAO nomination; Chairman Paul said the process is no different than before. As of this writing, the committee has not posted a date for a vote.

The timeline

  1. Jan. 2025From this month on, CISA has no Senate-confirmed director.
  2. Sept. 2025OMB Director Russell Vought calls the GAO "something that shouldn't exist," as reported by Axios.
  3. Nov. 19, 2026Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee hearing on thirteen nominations, including CISA and the GAO.
  4. Nov. 20, 2026Hearing record closes at 5 p.m. for written statements and questions.

Open questions

  1. When will the committee vote? It has not posted a date; The Hill, citing Politico, reports Chairman Paul said a vote on Mr. LoSapio will be held in early December. No date has been found for Mr. Ueland.
  2. Will the announced holds be placed? Sen. Ron Wyden told Nextgov he will place one on Mr. LoSapio's nomination; Sen. Thom Tillis told reporters he plans one, Federal News Network reports. No formal notice has been seen in the Senate record.
  3. Will the nominations be confirmed before the session ends? Under Senate rules, nominations not confirmed when a session ends can be returned to the President.
  4. Will the written questions and answers be published, and will the White House or either nominee respond?

Sources & method

How a file is built

Every video follows the same discipline. The structure changes with the story. The standard does not.

  1. The record

    We start from primary material: committee video, court orders, official reports and transcripts.

  2. The exhibits

    Each piece of evidence gets a letter. You always know what you are looking at and where it came from.

  3. The timeline

    Dates go on one line, so the sequence of events is clear before anyone interprets it.

  4. The open questions

    Where the record stops, we say so. Allegations are called allegations.
