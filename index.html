Jack Smith's Volume Two: The Senate Committee Vote That Didn't Happen

Special Counsel Jack Smith's final report came in two volumes. The first was released. The second, on the classified documents case, was not: a federal judge barred the Justice Department from sharing it. On November 10, 2026, the chairman of the Senate Judiciary Committee ruled a request to vote on a subpoena for it out of order.

  1. Exhibit A

    The Promise

    Sept. 29: the report's author testifies under oath, and says he will not describe it.

  2. Exhibit B

    The Motion

    Nov. 10: a senator moves for a vote on a subpoena for Volume Two.

  3. Exhibit C

    The Ruling

    The chairman rules the request out of order, and gives his reasons.

  4. Exhibit D

    The Rule Book

    The senator answers with the committee's own rules.

  5. Exhibit E

    The Answer

    No vote is held.

The timeline

  1. August 2022The FBI searches Mar-a-Lago.
  2. June 2023The indictment.
  3. July 2024Judge Cannon dismisses the case.
  4. November 2024Donald Trump wins the presidency. The appeal against him is dropped.
  5. January 2025Volume One is released. Volume Two is blocked.
  6. Sept. 29, 2026Jack Smith testifies, but declines to discuss Volume Two, citing the court order.
  7. Nov. 3, 2026Democrats win control of the Senate.
  8. Nov. 10, 2026The request for a vote is ruled out of order.
  9. January 2027A new Senate is sworn in. The Eleventh Circuit is set to hear arguments over the order on January 12, according to the committee chairman.

Open questions

  1. The chairman says the full report would be filled with one-sided allegations. If that is true, what is the argument against letting the public read it, and then answering it?
  2. The presumption of innocence is real, and it matters. Earlier special counsel reports, on the Russia investigation and on President Biden's handling of classified documents, were made public. Judge Cannon drew a line here because charges were filed and never resolved. Can that line be debated without seeing what is on the other side of it?
  3. The report was written by federal prosecutors, about evidence gathered by federal agents, and paid for by the public. What would it take for the public to be allowed to read it?
  4. If Volume Two holds nothing new, releasing it would cost the public nothing. A federal judge has ruled that it would cost the defendants something. That is the line the appeals court will be asked to test.

Sources & method

How a file is built

Every video follows the same discipline. The structure changes with the story. The standard does not.

  1. The record

    We start from primary material: committee video, court orders, official reports and transcripts.

  2. The exhibits

    Each piece of evidence gets a letter. You always know what you are looking at and where it came from.

  3. The timeline

    Dates go on one line, so the sequence of events is clear before anyone interprets it.

  4. The open questions

    Where the record stops, we say so. Allegations are called allegations.
