Sources & method

How a file is built

Every video follows the same discipline. The structure changes with the story. The standard does not.

The method

Four steps, every time

  1. The record

    We start from primary material: committee video, court orders, official reports and transcripts.

  2. The exhibits

    Each piece of evidence gets a letter. You always know what you are looking at and where it came from.

  3. The timeline

    Dates go on one line, so the sequence of events is clear before anyone interprets it.

  4. The open questions

    Where the record stops, we say so. Allegations are called allegations.

Sourcing rules

What we hold ourselves to

  • Primary records first. Committee video, court orders, indictments, official reports and transcripts come before anyone's summary of them.
  • Quotes are checked. Every quote is compared with the recording or the transcript. If we cannot verify it, we do not use it.
  • Allegations stay allegations. Charges in an indictment are described as what prosecutors alleged, not as established fact.
  • Claims are attributed. When a senator or an official makes a claim, we say who made it and where.
  • Uncertain facts are flagged or left out. If sources disagree on a number or a date, we say so or leave it out.
  • No fabricated footage. We do not stage hearings, invent dialogue or alter what officials said.

Footage

Where it comes from

The footage in our videos comes from official sources: the channels of members of Congress, federal agencies such as the Department of Justice, and the Architect of the Capitol.

Clips of hearings are shown so you can hear officials in their own words. The narration around them is our own analysis and commentary on the public record.

Each case file lists the specific records it was built from, so you can check our work.
