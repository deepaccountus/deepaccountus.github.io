FEMA Chief Asked Why Illinois Was Denied: "At His Discretion"

On Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026, the House Homeland Security Committee's Subcommittee on Emergency Management and Technology held a hearing titled "Beyond the Storm: Assessing FEMA's Mission Readiness." The only witness was FEMA Administrator Cameron Hamilton, who led the agency in an acting role in early 2025, was removed that May, and was confirmed by the Senate on Aug. 7, 2026. Rep. Delia Ramirez (D-Ill.) asked him why Illinois was denied a major disaster declaration. He answered that Stafford Act declarations "are at his discretion," meaning the president's. The dollar figures she cited, $27.6 million in costs against FEMA's $24.9 million mark, belong to Illinois' request after its August 2026 storm; the counties she named belong to an earlier storm, in August 2025. FEMA denied the 2026 request in a letter dated Oct. 7; the state says it gave no specific reason. Illinois filed its appeal on Nov. 5, and as of Nov. 25 we found no decision. The law says the president "may declare" a major disaster. FEMA's regulation uses a per-resident figure, $1.94 for disasters starting on or after Oct. 1, 2025, as an "indicator" that a disaster "might warrant" federal help, alongside other factors. The hearing also covered the February snowstorm requests from New York and New Jersey, Hamilton's view that the federal government has too often been treated as a default financial backstop, the BRIC program and a federal court ruling, the Government Accountability Office's counts of FEMA departures, flood insurance premiums, and two things that run out on Dec. 11 unless Congress acts: government funding and the flood insurance program's authority to write new policies. This file does not rule on who is right: it sets out what was said, what the documents say, and what is still open.

The timeline

  1. Early 2025Cameron Hamilton begins leading FEMA in an acting role; he is removed that May.
  2. April 2025FEMA announces it is ending the BRIC program and says about $882 million would go back to the Treasury or be reassigned by Congress; 20 states later sue.
  3. August 2025Storms hit Boone, Cook, Kane, McHenry and Will counties in Illinois; the federal request is denied that fall.
  4. December 2025A federal judge rules that the administration unlawfully terminated BRIC.
  5. Feb. 7, 2026FEMA denies Illinois' appeal on the 2025 storm, a denial the state says covered aid to individual residents.
  6. March 2026The judge orders FEMA to follow the BRIC ruling; FEMA later offers $1 billion in new grants.
  7. May 2026A council the president set up reports; one option raises the per-resident figure from $1.94 to $2.99.
  8. July 2, 2026FEMA first denies New York's request for its February snowstorm; New York appeals on July 31.
  9. Aug. 4, 2026The Government Accountability Office reports on FEMA's workforce and its rescinded strategic plan.
  10. Aug. 7, 2026The Senate confirms Hamilton as FEMA Administrator.
  11. August 2026Storms hit eight Illinois counties; Indiana receives a major disaster declaration on Aug. 25.
  12. Sept. 2, 2026New York's governor says FEMA has denied the state's appeal over the February 2026 blizzard.
  13. Oct. 7, 2026FEMA denies Illinois' request for the August 2026 storm; the state says the letter gave no specific reason.
  14. Nov. 5, 2026Illinois files its appeal; it says FEMA has since validated $43 million in eligible costs, up from $27.6 million in the original request, against a $24.9 million mark.
  15. Nov. 19, 2026House Homeland Security subcommittee hearing with Hamilton as the only witness.
  16. Dec. 11, 2026Government funding and the flood insurance program's authority to write new policies run out unless Congress acts; existing policies stay in force until their own end dates.

Open questions

  1. Will FEMA decide Illinois' appeal on the August 2026 storm? Illinois filed it on Nov. 5, 2026; as of Nov. 25, 2026, we found no decision.
  2. Will Congress act by Dec. 11, 2026, on government funding and the flood insurance program's authority to write new policies? Existing policies would stay in force until their own end dates.
  3. Will FEMA publish its strategic plan, and say how many people it needs? Hamilton gave no date and no number at the hearing; he said a workforce analysis is underway.
  4. Will the hearing record be published? The chairman said it would stay open for 10 days for written questions.
  5. Should passing the mark mean a state gets help, or should the president decide each time?

Sources & method

How a file is built

Every video follows the same discipline. The structure changes with the story. The standard does not.

  1. The record

    We start from primary material: committee video, court orders, official reports and transcripts.

  2. The exhibits

    Each piece of evidence gets a letter. You always know what you are looking at and where it came from.

  3. The timeline

    Dates go on one line, so the sequence of events is clear before anyone interprets it.

  4. The open questions

    Where the record stops, we say so. Allegations are called allegations.
