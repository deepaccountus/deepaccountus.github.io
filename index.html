What Stopped U.S. Flights? Four Explanations in Four Days

On Friday, Nov. 13, 2026, at about 3:30 p.m. Eastern, the FAA's air traffic network became unstable, and the agency stopped departures across the United States for more than four hours. About 3,500 aircraft in the air landed safely. Over the next four days the cause was described four ways: old equipment overloaded, with the new equipment holding up (Secretary Duffy, Friday); new equipment overwhelming the older system (Secretary Mullin, Friday); by the FAA's initial investigation, a new router at a remote site flooding an old network (Secretary Duffy on Saturday, the FAA in writing on Sunday); and old equipment plus copper-to-fiber work under the previous administration (President Trump, Monday). The first three are not the same explanation, but they can partly fit together; the record does not yet show which work the president is referring to. The FAA's review of the root cause is still open.

The timeline

  1. Jan. 11, 2023FAA pauses domestic departures after the NOTAM system outage; the FAA announces it lifted at 8:50 a.m. Eastern.
  2. Sept. 2026The administration asks Congress for about $30 billion more for air traffic control.
  3. Nov. 13, 2026About 3:30 p.m. Eastern: the FAA network becomes unstable; nationwide ground stop. 8:15 p.m.: flights resume. About 8:30 p.m.: Secretaries Mullin and Duffy brief reporters at the White House.
  4. Nov. 14, 2026Secretary Duffy points to a router installed by the contractor; L3Harris issues a statement.
  5. Nov. 15, 2026President Trump posts twice on Truth Social. The FAA publishes its written account.
  6. Nov. 16, 2026President Trump answers reporters at the White House. Senate Republicans question the $50 billion request.
  7. Nov. 17, 2026Per ABC News: the department submits a $50.1 billion request; 13 Senate Commerce Democrats send a letter; the FAA halts router installation.
  8. NextIf the FAA publishes its root-cause findings, this file will put them next to these four explanations, word by word.

Open questions

  1. Was it a faulty router, or the way it was installed?
  2. Why did one or two remote sites affect most of the country?
  3. Will the money, $30 billion or $50 billion, be approved, and with what conditions?

Sources & method

How a file is built

Every video follows the same discipline. The structure changes with the story. The standard does not.

  1. The record

    We start from primary material: committee video, court orders, official reports and transcripts.

  2. The exhibits

    Each piece of evidence gets a letter. You always know what you are looking at and where it came from.

  3. The timeline

    Dates go on one line, so the sequence of events is clear before anyone interprets it.

  4. The open questions

    Where the record stops, we say so. Allegations are called allegations.
