Case File #010

"Did Oracle Keep the Promise?" Oracle CEO Sicilia on the VA Contract

On Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026, the Senate Veterans' Affairs Committee, chaired by Sen. Jerry Moran (R-Kan.), with Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.) as ranking member, heard from Dr. Neil Evans, who runs the VA's health record program; Mike Sicilia, one of Oracle's two chief executives, with Oracle executive Seema Verma; and Carol Harris of the GAO. On Thursday, Nov. 19, the House Veterans' Affairs Committee, chaired by Rep. Mike Bost (R-Ill.), with Rep. Mark Takano (D-Calif.) as ranking member, heard from Mr. Sicilia, appearing under a congressional subpoena, and Ms. Verma. The subject: the electronic health record the VA contracted for in 2018 with Cerner, which Oracle bought in 2022. In August 2026, the VA raised the contract's ceiling, the most the VA may order, from just under $10 billion to about $26.94 billion. A ceiling is not a bill: the work is bought through task orders, each at a fixed price. Asked by Rep. Maggie Goodlander (D-N.H.) whether Oracle kept the promise it made to the committee in 2022, Mr. Sicilia said he has "explained many times why I believe yes," adding that he "can't do the question justice with yes or no." He said the scope of the work had changed. As of Nov. 21, 2026, a House subpoena calls for Oracle's chairman, Larry Ellison, to appear on Dec. 10, according to the committee's ranking member; the committee's public calendar lists no hearing for that date.

The evidence

Exhibits

  1. Exhibit A

    Two Rooms, One Week

    Wednesday, Nov. 18: the Senate Veterans' Affairs Committee, with Chairman Moran and Ranking Member Blumenthal. Three witnesses: Dr. Evans, program executive director of the VA's health record integration office; Mr. Sicilia, with Ms. Verma; and Ms. Harris, director for information technology and cybersecurity at the GAO, Congress's auditor. Thursday, Nov. 19: the House Veterans' Affairs Committee, with Mr. Sicilia and Ms. Verma. Chairman Bost said this year's deployments "have gone far more smoothly than those that came before," and that "the veterans and taxpayers deserve to understand why that price tag keeps changing." Ranking Member Takano told the witnesses: "Having to compel your testimony through a subpoena is absolutely unacceptable." Oracle withdrew from a House hearing held on Sept. 2, 2026. That day the committee voted 19 to 0, on a motion by Rep. Maxine Dexter (D-Ore.), to subpoena Mr. Sicilia and Mr. Ellison, Oracle's chairman and co-founder. Mr. Sicilia's House appearance was under that subpoena; Ms. Verma accompanied him.

  2. Exhibit B

    The Numbers, From the Documents

    The VA signed the contract in 2018 with Cerner. It has a ceiling: the most the VA may order. The work is bought through task orders, each at a fixed price. Per Oracle's written testimony, its final proposal was an increase of $16.9 billion; the VA awarded it on Aug. 18, 2026, and announced it on Aug. 19. The ceiling went from just under $10 billion to about $26.94 billion, and the end date from 2028 to May 2031. Sen. Jim Banks (R-Ind.) asked how many more ceiling increases the VA expects. Dr. Evans: "None." The ceiling, he said, is "an authorization to spend"; the dollars still have to be appropriated by Congress. Chairman Moran said the committee "has no answers on what this program will cost over its full life" and asked the VA for an updated, independent life-cycle cost estimate. The program estimates are the VA's own. In September 2025, the VA gave Congress an estimate of $37.2 billion to deploy and run the system through 2031; Dr. Evans said the VA has "high confidence" in it, within 5 percent of a 2022 independent estimate. Ms. Harris described the VA's full life-cycle figure: that, plus $19.3 billion to operate the system ten more years, $56.5 billion in all. Asked about it, she said: "I'm not confident that the $56.5 billion estimate is realistic," largely because the GAO has not seen the documentation behind it. She called the VA's inflation assumptions unrealistic and the 2022 estimate no longer current. The system is live at 19 of 170 VA medical centers; 26 more are planned for 2027. The VA and Oracle say the original $10 billion covers about fifty.

  3. Exhibit C

    Two Statements From 2022

    In July 2022, about a month after Oracle bought Cerner, Mr. Sicilia testified twice. To the Senate committee (July 20), as Sen. Blumenthal read it back, Oracle would deliver "without exceeding the contracted cost" and fix issues "at our expense." To a House subcommittee (July 27), per Oracle's written testimony, he said Oracle would deliver "for the amounts contemplated in the current contract under the current scope"; "if there are big new pieces of functionality not included in the scope, that's a different discussion"; and "if there are significant cost overruns," Oracle was "prepared to bear those costs." Rep. Goodlander read that last line and asked: "Did Oracle keep the promise that you made to this committee in 2022, yes or no?" Mr. Sicilia: he has "explained many times why I believe yes," adding that he "can't do the question justice with yes or no," and later: "I don't believe the promise was broken." He pointed to the condition he had also stated, "unless there was a significant change in scope." He told the Senate the scope had drastically changed. His written testimony: interfaces to other systems from 57 to 164; concurrent users from 85,000 to 195,000; record capacity from 8 million to 26.1 million. To Rep. Jack Bergman (R-Mich.) he compared it to planning a three-story office building and needing a nine-story one, and said: "The only commitment to spend comes in the course of task orders." Oracle's reading: the pledge covered the original scope, and Oracle still absorbs overruns on fixed-price orders. The reading of Rep. Goodlander and Sen. Blumenthal: a promise to stay within the original price. Chairman Bost described the pledge as covering overruns "within the scope of the contract." Both texts are here; the verdict is yours.

  4. Exhibit D

    How Congress Found Out

    Sen. Blumenthal: "Congress learned of the new $27 billion contract ceiling through news reports, not from the VA, not from Oracle." Dr. Evans, in reply, said the VA shared its program cost estimate with the committee in September 2025. Asked whether there was a decision not to notify Congress, he said: "There was not an intentional decision to not notify Congress." Asked whether it was inadvertent: "Correct." Oracle's written testimony says the VA controlled the public announcement and the notice to Congress. A second gap is documents for the auditors. Asked by Sen. Mazie Hirono (D-Hawaii) when the GAO requested the VA's cost analysis, Ms. Harris said: "Well over a year ago." Chairman Moran asked: "Why does it take a process, an agreement?" Dr. Evans: "We will." He said he and Ms. Harris had just discussed it.

  5. Exhibit E

    Execution, Platform, or Contract

    Dr. Evans told the Senate: "This program is back on track." Thirteen medical centers went live this year on schedule, and Ms. Harris credited the program for it. Senators in both parties looked at the years before. Sen. Tim Sheehy (R-Mont.) said it has "been a disgrace how long this has taken." Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) said it "has all the hallmarks of a failed execution," setting $10 billion against $37 billion; those measure different things, the old contract ceiling and the VA's estimate for the whole program. His conclusion: "This is an execution fail. It's not a platform fail." Sen. Angus King (I-Maine), who caucuses with Democrats, questioned the contract itself: "It sounds like a license to print money." Dr. Evans said each task order carries a firm fixed price. Sen. King compared the VA with Kaiser Permanente, which uses a different vendor, Epic; the per-patient figures were his, and we could not verify them. He asked for the comparison "apples to apples." In the House, Rep. Derrick Van Orden (R-Wis.) said: "This contract needs to be severed immediately." Mr. Sicilia, earlier that morning: "I'm not here to milk the taxpayers or the veterans for 17 billion dollars"; if the government sees no value, he said, it should "by no means continue to fund the program."

  6. Exhibit F

    The Political Question

    In his opening statement, Sen. Blumenthal said Oracle's chairman, Larry Ellison, "is reportedly a $45 million backer of the President's political network." "Reportedly" is his word; the figure traces to press reports citing unnamed sources, and we have not verified it. He later showed a photograph of President Trump and Mr. Ellison. Mr. Sicilia said: "I did not discuss Mr. Ellison's political donations with him." In the House, answering Chairman Bost, he said Mr. Ellison "had absolutely nothing to do with the VA contract" and that he had never talked with anybody in the White House about it. Oracle's written testimony: "Neither Oracle's founder, Larry Ellison, nor I participated in or sought to influence the negotiations related to the EHRM contract extension." Earlier in the House hearing, after Rep. Dexter raised Mr. Ellison's ties to the President, Chairman Bost reminded members not to deal in "personalities of the president," and said many members on the dais had taken the same political contributions. Asked by Ranking Member Takano whether he had asked Mr. Ellison directly, Mr. Sicilia said he had not; Oracle, he said, ran an internal investigation and told the committee in writing that Mr. Ellison was not involved. As of Nov. 21, 2026, we have found no White House statement on these questions.

Charges in an indictment are allegations, not findings of guilt. Statements by senators and officials are reported as their statements.

Open questions

  1. Will Larry Ellison appear on Dec. 10? As of Nov. 21, 2026, the House subpoena calls for him to appear that day, according to the committee's ranking member; the committee's public calendar lists no hearing for that date.
  2. Will the GAO receive the analysis behind the VA's cost estimate? As of Nov. 21, 2026, the VA says it will provide it; we have not seen the GAO say it has received it.
  3. Will the written answers arrive? As of Nov. 21, 2026, Ms. Verma has promised Rep. Nancy Mace (R-S.C.) exact counts of open trouble tickets, and the Senate record is open for a week for written questions.
  4. Will Oracle's commitment hold? As of Nov. 21, 2026: asked by Ranking Member Takano, Mr. Sicilia said Oracle will keep the health record business through 2031, "if it pleases Congress I'll make that commitment."
  5. What happens in January? As of Nov. 21, 2026, the 26 deployments planned for 2027 begin in January, the month a new Congress convenes.
