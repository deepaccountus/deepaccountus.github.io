VA Psychedelic Therapy: Est. 180,000 Eligible, 10 Sites Not Announced

On Wednesday, Nov. 18, 2026, the House Veterans' Affairs Subcommittee on Health, chaired by Rep. Mariannette Miller-Meeks (R-Iowa), heard from the Department of Veterans Affairs and four outside experts on psychedelic-assisted therapy for veterans. Neither MDMA nor psilocybin is approved by the FDA for any use, and federal rules list both in Schedule I. Andrew Gradison, a senior advisor at the Veterans Health Administration, said about 6.4 million veterans actively use VA care and roughly 2.1 million have been diagnosed with treatment-resistant depression, major depressive disorder or post-traumatic stress disorder; applying study criteria, he said roughly 180,000 might be eligible. He told Rep. Maggie Goodlander (D-N.H.) that is his estimate, not a firm number; it is not in his written testimony. The VA's written testimony says it has identified ten facilities to serve as early implementers, educators or sites-in-training. The VA has not named them in public. At the hearing it agreed to give the committee the list and a staffing plan with costs, and Goodlander asked for them within 30 days. The VA said its fiscal year 2027 budget did not include a request for specific funding for this work. Members raised concerns about private clinics and about VA staffing. As of Nov. 28, we found no FDA approval of a classic psychedelic, and neither the site list nor the staffing plan has been made public.

The timeline

  1. Aug. 2024The FDA declines to approve an earlier application for MDMA-assisted therapy.
  2. Apr. 18, 2026President Trump signs Executive Order 14401, "Accelerating Medical Treatments for Serious Mental Illness."
  3. July 13, 2026The VA and the Department of Health and Human Services sign a five-year agreement on research and data sharing.
  4. Aug. 5, 2026The VA announces a clinical trial of psilocybin for major depressive disorder in veterans.
  5. Sept. 30, 2026The VA releases its 2026 national veteran suicide prevention annual report, with data through 2024.
  6. Oct. 2026At a conference in New York, a VA official says it would be a challenge to treat four digits' worth of patients in the first year, as reported by Politico and quoted by Newsweek.
  7. Nov. 18, 2026House Veterans' Affairs Subcommittee on Health hearing on psychedelic-assisted therapies for veterans; the VA agrees to give the committee its list of ten sites and a staffing plan.
  8. Dec. 18, 2026Thirty days after the hearing: the date by which Rep. Goodlander asked for the site list and the staffing plan.

Open questions

  1. Will the VA give the committee its list of ten sites and its staffing plan, and will either be made public? As of Nov. 28, 2026, neither is public.
  2. Will the FDA approve a psychedelic therapy? As of Nov. 28, 2026, no classic psychedelic, and neither MDMA nor psilocybin, is approved.
  3. Who would be treated first? Asked, Gradison said the VA would follow principles of medical ethics; he did not describe a priority rule.
  4. Will the VA's budget include funding for this work? The fiscal year 2027 budget did not include a request for specific funding.
  5. If a treatment is approved and, as a VA official reportedly said in October, even a four-digit number of patients would be a challenge in the first year, who should decide who goes first?

Sources & method

How a file is built

Every video follows the same discipline. The structure changes with the story. The standard does not.

  1. The record

    We start from primary material: committee video, court orders, official reports and transcripts.

  2. The exhibits

    Each piece of evidence gets a letter. You always know what you are looking at and where it came from.

  3. The timeline

    Dates go on one line, so the sequence of events is clear before anyone interprets it.

  4. The open questions

    Where the record stops, we say so. Allegations are called allegations.
