The Senators Weren't Told: Jack Smith and Their Phone Toll Records

Jack Smith's office subpoenaed the phone toll records of Republican members of Congress: records of who called whom, not what was said. At prosecutors' request, court orders barred the phone companies from telling them. At a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing on September 29, 2026, senators and Mr. Smith disagreed about why the secrecy was needed and whether it was lawful. The senators' own counts of the members affected do not match.

  1. Exhibit A

    The Accusation

    The chairman's two claims: toll records, and the content of messages.

  2. Exhibit B

    The Answer

    Jack Smith: non-content records, approved after consulting the Public Integrity Section.

  3. Exhibit C

    The Secrecy Orders

    Court orders barred the phone companies from telling anyone. The judges were not told whose records they were.

  4. Exhibit D

    The Law

    Two objections: the speech or debate clause, and a statute on Senate office data.

  5. Exhibit E

    The Comparable

    Democrats point to the same method used during the first Trump administration.

The timeline

  1. 2017 to 2020Leak investigations in which the Justice Department obtained records of two members of Congress, both Democrats, and 43 congressional staff members from both parties, along with non-disclosure orders. The inspector general later reviewed them.
  2. Dec. 27, 2020Congress amends 2 U.S.C. § 6628: the Senate Sergeant at Arms, and providers serving a Senate office, cannot be barred by a court order from notifying that office of legal process seeking its data.
  3. November 2022Jack Smith is appointed special counsel.
  4. Dec. 10, 2024The Justice Department's inspector general releases its report on the 2017 to 2020 leak investigations.
  5. Sept. 29, 2026Jack Smith testifies before the Senate Judiciary Committee about the toll-record subpoenas.
  6. NextThe chairman says he has released records and will release more. As the documents are released, this file will read them against the testimony.

Open questions

  1. Should prosecutors have to tell a judge when the records they want kept secret belong to members of Congress, no matter which party runs the Justice Department?
  2. The speech or debate clause protects lawmakers from being questioned elsewhere about their legislative work. Does a list of who a senator called, without a word of what was said, cross that line?
  3. Both parties have now been on the receiving end of this method. What rule would each of them accept if they knew the other side would be the one using it?

Sources & method

How a file is built

Every video follows the same discipline. The structure changes with the story. The standard does not.

  1. The record

    We start from primary material: committee video, court orders, official reports and transcripts.

  2. The exhibits

    Each piece of evidence gets a letter. You always know what you are looking at and where it came from.

  3. The timeline

    Dates go on one line, so the sequence of events is clear before anyone interprets it.

  4. The open questions

    Where the record stops, we say so. Allegations are called allegations.
