Case File #007

A Week of Outages: What Officials Told Congress Under Oath

From Nov. 12 to Nov. 17, 2026, a series of outages and disruptions affected Microsoft 365, JPMorgan Chase, United Airlines, Apple and an internal NSA network, and the FAA held every departing flight in the country. Most organizations that explained their outages gave a technical cause of their own: a software defect, a software bug in hardware systems, an internal update, routine maintenance; for the ground stop, the Transportation Secretary described overloaded routers and old hardware. None, in its own statement as reported, described an attack. On Nov. 18, at the House Homeland Security Committee's annual worldwide threats hearing, Rep. Michael Guest (R-Miss.) asked whether the outages were related. Under oath, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said, "We have seen nothing that would indicate that they are related"; Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said, "we have no indication that these incidents are related"; and FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia said, "At this point, there is no indication whatsoever that there is any cyber malicious activity related to any of those incidents." "No indication" is what investigators have found so far; none of them called it a final report. Still open: the FAA's investigation of the ground stop, Microsoft's final report, the full cause of the NSA outage, and United's cause.

The evidence

Exhibits

  1. Exhibit A

    The Six Days

    Thu., Nov. 12: Microsoft 365 began failing late that morning, Eastern time, and was not fully restored until the next morning; in a preliminary report quoted by BleepingComputer, Microsoft blamed a software defect introduced months earlier, triggered by a surge of traffic while part of its capacity was offline. Fri., Nov. 13: the FAA held every departing flight in the country for more than four hours; the advisory, as published by FOX 5 DC, read: "Due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety." Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said routers were overloaded with data and old hardware went down; the FAA's contractor, L3Harris, said it is assisting the FAA's investigation. The payroll and HR service Workday also reported a disruption that week. Mon., Nov. 16: customers of JPMorgan Chase reported trouble with online banking; Chase said, as quoted by Just The News, that it "experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems" and that "there is no evidence this is cyber-related"; Bank of America told CBS News its systems "were operating normally." United Airlines held its departures for more than 90 minutes and said, according to the AP, there was "no indication that this was a cybersecurity-related issue." Tue., Nov. 17: Apple said, in a statement relayed by Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, that "an internal update caused some outages"; the NSA told Al Jazeera it "experienced temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance."

  2. Exhibit B

    Before the Hearing

    On Fox News on Sunday, Nov. 15, Secretary Mullin said of the ground stop, according to a transcript published by the Daily Beast: "It really appears to be, with 98 percent certainty," that the cause was "a 50-year-old system." On Hannity on Monday night, Nov. 16, according to Breitbart's write-up, he described a conversation about the outages and relayed a theory he said "kind of makes sense" to him. He said the person told him, "I believe that it's the SI" (a term Fortune reported as short for super intelligence), and that older "backup systems are overwhelmed by the new systems now." He said CISA had told him it was "not seeing any traffic that would make us think that this is a cyberattack."

  3. Exhibit C

    The Hearing and the Question

    Chairman Andrew Garbarino (R-N.Y.) raised the outages in his opening statement: "While there is no indication that these incidents were caused by cyber attacks, they underscore the importance of securing our networks and strengthening their resilience." In his opening statement, DNI Jay Clayton said: "based on what we know today, after a great deal of diligence, these are separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes. They are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack." Rep. Michael Guest (R-Miss.) then asked all three witnesses whether the outages were related. Secretary Mullin, Director Clayton and FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia each said there was no indication of a link or of an attack.

  4. Exhibit D

    The NSA Exchange

    Rep. Dan Goldman (D-N.Y.), who said this was his last hearing on the committee, asked Director Clayton whether the NSA's problems "on Monday" came from internal software and technological upgrades; the agency's outage was reported on Tuesday. Clayton said he also needed to protect the agency and answered in general terms: "Patching, upgrading, replacing hardware... you take systems down, you bring them back up." He began to say the issue, "as far as we can tell," was "exactly that type of routine" work before Goldman cut in: "you're clearly not answering my direct question." Clayton: "I'm actually answering the question in the most appropriate way I can."

  5. Exhibit E

    "98 Percent"

    Asked by Rep. Eli Crane (R-Ariz.) whether he had ever seen this many outages this close together, Secretary Mullin said, "Absolutely not," and said DHS was convening a call with industry, suggesting companies warn customers before upgrades. Later, asked by another member whether a connection had been ruled out or simply not found, Director Clayton said the outages had different causes, and that investigators looked for the techniques used in past attacks and did not see them. Asked by the same member how confident he was, on a scale of one to 10, Secretary Mullin said: "I'm 98% confident that this has nothing to do with our adversaries hacking or going after our systems." The hearing record does not show how that figure was calculated.

  6. Exhibit F

    The Empty Chair

    Chairman Garbarino said: "I am disappointed that Director Patel chose not to appear before the Committee today. Today's hearing date was first provided to Director Patel in August." Ranking Member Bennie Thompson (D-Miss.) said Patel's "absence for a second consecutive year limits the committee's ability to conduct effective oversight at this hearing." The chairman thanked Deputy Director Raia for appearing and said the committee's issue was not with him. We could not confirm the FBI's explanation for the Director's absence in the FBI's own words; if the FBI provides one, we will add it to this file.

  7. Exhibit G

    The Football Game

    On Saturday, Nov. 14, the day after the ground stop, Secretary Mullin flew on a government plane to the LSU-Texas football game. Ranking Member Thompson raised it at the hearing; in his words, "the secretary boarded a luxury jet, flew to a college football game." The Secretary answered that the trip had been planned for weeks and that canceling "could bring confusion" and "show maybe distrust that we're not even willing to fly." He said he is required to fly on a government plane, that he reimbursed the government, and that he paid for the tickets himself. DHS told ABC News that "any flight not related to his service as DHS Secretary he pays for personally," and the Secretary told CNBC on Nov. 17 that he "would have never went if the airways weren't completely open."

Charges in an indictment are allegations, not findings of guilt. Statements by senators and officials are reported as their statements.

The timeline

  1. Nov. 12, 2026Microsoft 365 outage begins; restored the next morning.
  2. Nov. 13, 2026FAA nationwide ground stop, more than four hours. Workday also reports a disruption.
  3. Nov. 14, 2026Secretary Mullin flies on a government plane to the LSU-Texas game.
  4. Nov. 15-16, 2026Secretary Mullin on Fox News: "98 percent certainty"; on Hannity, the "SI" theory.
  5. Nov. 16, 2026JPMorgan Chase online banking issue; United Airlines departures held.
  6. Nov. 17, 2026Apple outage ("an internal update"); NSA internal network degradation ("scheduled routine maintenance").
  7. Nov. 18, 2026House Homeland Security Committee hearing: "no indication" of a link or an attack.

  2. What will Microsoft's final report say?
  3. What exactly caused the NSA outage, and what caused United's?
